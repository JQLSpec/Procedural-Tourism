# Procedural Tourism: A Cooperative Travel Game
## Player Rulebook

**Version 1.1 · Playtest rules**

*Branding revision: official title adopted; gameplay and card identifiers unchanged.*

Explore somewhere new, discover something surprising, and help your travel companions do the same. This cooperative game turns a day abroad (or closer to home) into an improvised adventure. You are not racing each other. Your shared goal is to complete your personal quests while navigating a common condition and your own curses.

## 1. What you need

- A Quest deck, a Condition deck and a Curse deck (physical cards or digital equivalents). A playable subset of each is sufficient for testing.
- Two or more travelers; the initial game is designed around two.
- A way to track the round's start and end times and, optionally, record discoveries.
- Normal travel essentials, including access to maps, translation, money and emergency communications as needed.

There is no game master, board, mandatory scoring system or required purchase beyond ordinary travel needs. Agree on a modest spending limit and personal accessibility or dietary needs before play. Set aside cards clearly unsuitable for your destination.

## 2. The three decks

| Card | Who draws? | What does it do? |
|---|---|---|
| Quest | Each traveler | Gives you an individual objective to explore or learn something |
| Condition | The group together | Changes how everyone approaches the adventure |
| Curse | Each traveler | Gives you a personal complication to work around |

Quests are designed to take 90 minutes to four hours of meaningful effort. Conditions can restrict, require or permit behaviors. Curses are part of every round, not a punishment for failure. A curse should be a manageable inconvenience rather than an ordeal.

## 3. Starting a round

A round lasts **12 consecutive hours**. Agree on a start time, such as 9 AM, and an end time 12 hours later (9 PM). You need not play every day or spend the entire round pursuing quests.

1. Shuffle the three decks separately (or randomize digital cards).
2. Each traveler draws one Quest and reads it aloud.
3. Draw one shared Condition and read it aloud.
4. Each traveler draws one Curse and reads it aloud.
5. Check that the cards can work together using the exchange rules in section 7.
6. Plan your adventure together and begin.

For two travelers, five cards are active: two Quests, one Condition and two Curses. Keep them visible or accessible throughout the round. In later rounds draw from the remaining cards rather than reshuffling everything; when a deck runs out, shuffle its discarded cards. Never shuffle an active card back into the deck.

## 4. Completing your quest

Follow the card's objective and completion requirements. Spend at least 90 minutes of genuine effort, including relevant travel, exploration, research and documentation. Four hours is the intended base quest effort, not a separate countdown. If you finish the written requirements early, explore further or deepen your learning until you meet the minimum. Idle waiting and deliberate time padding do not count.

You may work on both travelers' quests during one outing, and the same activity may contribute to multiple quests if it satisfies each card's requirements. You may also split up by mutual agreement, unless an active card requires you to stay together. Each quest holder must personally participate and be able to explain what they discovered; your companion cannot simply do the whole quest for you.

Only activities after the draw count. Ordinary vacation plans can count if they meet the quest requirements. Evidence may be notes, permitted photos, maps, sketches, tickets, receipts or a credible oral account. No social media post is necessary.

Maps, translation tools, internet research and AI assistance are permitted unless restricted by a card. Explore firsthand; online research alone is not a substitute for visiting or experiencing the relevant places.

## 5. Following conditions and curses

### Conditions

The shared condition applies to every traveler, even if you pursue quests separately. Check the scope printed on the card:

- **Full Round:** applies throughout the 12-hour window, including ordinary leisure activities, subject to exemptions.
- **Quest Activities:** applies while actively planning or pursuing quests and during relevant local travel. It does not restrict unrelated leisure or existing commitments.

Some conditions are beneficial, permitting a redraw, a substitution or suspension of a curse. Follow the precise wording of the card.

### Curses

Your curse applies only to you. Your companions may help unless a card prohibits it; their assistance must comply with their own curse and the shared condition. You cannot transfer your curse. You never have more than one active curse.

| Duration | When the curse ends |
|---|---|
| Persistent | At the end of the round (its scope may be limited to quest activities) |
| Conditional | When you complete the card's optional redemption requirement, or at round end |
| Quest-bound | When you complete your quest, or at round end |

A beneficial condition may explicitly suspend or remove a curse. Otherwise, you cannot simply pay, trade or agree your way out of one. All remaining curses expire at round end.

## 6. Vacation and safety come first

The game never overrides local law, safety, accessibility, health, dietary requirements, consent, privacy, essential communication or existing travel and work commitments. Flights, hotel transfers, booked reservations and essential navigation are exempt when necessary. You may decline an activity that crosses your boundaries, without a game penalty.

Respect private property, photography rules, cultural etiquette and the people around you. Do not pressure strangers to help. Use accessible alternatives, take breaks, and stop if weather, fatigue or circumstances make an activity unsuitable. Agree on spending; no one is obliged to make a purchase they cannot afford or do not want.

When a card would prevent essential activity, ignore that restriction for as long as necessary. Resume play when practical. If the card is no longer feasible, exchange it under the next section. You can abandon a round at any time.

## 7. Exchanging incompatible cards

An unusual or inconvenient card is part of the fun. An impossible, unsafe, redundant or unreasonable combination is not.

1. Apply the exemptions in section 6 first.
2. If a quest is infeasible on its own (for example, a necessary service is closed), exchange that quest.
3. If the shared condition prevents an otherwise feasible quest, exchange the condition for everyone.
4. If a curse duplicates the shared condition, conflicts with a quest or creates an unreasonable burden, exchange that curse.
5. If two curses are individually workable but conflict when combined, agree which one to exchange. If you cannot agree, choose randomly.
6. Draw a replacement from the same deck and check the combination again. Set exchanged cards aside with the discards.

These rules also apply if circumstances change during play. Keep any progress that satisfies the new requirements. Do not redraw merely to obtain a more attractive card. If no feasible combination remains, end or pause play rather than force the adventure.

## 8. Ending the round

You may finish your quests early and enjoy the rest of the round. Full-round effects continue until the agreed end time unless the card explicitly removes them. When the 12 hours are over, share discoveries and determine whether each traveler completed their quest while observing the applicable rules.

| Outcome | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Successful Expedition | Every traveler completed their quest |
| Partial Expedition | At least one, but not all, completed their quest |
| Misadventure | No traveler completed their quest |

Use good faith when deciding completion. Correct accidental rule breaches where practical. A material uncorrected breach prevents the responsible traveler's affected quest from counting as complete; if a card requires a collective result, evaluate that result together. Using a safety or essential-needs exemption is not a breach.

Discard the round's cards and expire all remaining effects. There are **no penalties for failure** and no additional curses in the next round. If you play again, everyone draws a fresh Quest and Curse and the group draws a new Condition.

An expedition journal is optional: record the date, place, cards, outcome, favorite discovery and funniest complication. There is no required score or winner.

## 9. Example round

Two travelers agree to play from 8 AM to 8 PM.

| | Traveler A | Traveler B |
|---|---|---|
| Quest | End of the Line: explore a transit terminus | Anatomy of a Dish: investigate and eat a regional dish |
| Curse | The Accidental Tourist: no personal map use during quest activities | The Untranslatable: no personal translation-app use during quest activities |
| Shared condition | Unreviewed: no online ratings or reviews for selecting quest destinations | Same condition |

Traveler B navigates to a suitable unfamiliar terminus while Traveler A translates signs and menus. Together they explore the neighborhood, visit a market, investigate a regional dish and eat lunch. They choose places based on firsthand impressions rather than reviews. Each records their own discoveries and checks the requirements on their quest card.

If both meet the requirements and minimum effort, they have a Successful Expedition. If only one succeeds, they have a Partial Expedition. Either way, the next round starts fresh.

## 10. Quick reference

**Before:** Agree on a 12-hour window, budget and any destination exclusions.

**Draw:** One Quest each → one shared Condition → one Curse each → resolve conflicts.

**Explore:** Cooperate, follow applicable cards, and spend at least 90 meaningful minutes on each quest. Take breaks and enjoy the destination.

**Finish:** Check requirements, share discoveries, record the outcome if desired, discard cards and expire effects.

**Remember:** Quests give you purpose. Conditions shape your environment. Curses challenge your ingenuity. The adventure is the reward.
